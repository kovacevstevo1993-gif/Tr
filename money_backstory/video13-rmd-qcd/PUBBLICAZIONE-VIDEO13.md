## Titolo
Every Retiree With an IRA Must Know This RMD Rule at 73 (One Move Cuts Your Tax)

## Descrizione
Your required minimum distribution (RMD) at 73 is taxable income, it can make your Social Security taxable, and missing it costs a 25% penalty. But the IRS also allows one move, the qualified charitable distribution (QCD), that cut Walter's federal tax from $2,675 to $586 in our example. In this video you will see the exact RMD formula, the first-year April 1 trap, and the QCD rules that trip people up. Every number comes from the IRS itself.

We follow two fictional people. Walter is 75, single, with an $800,000 traditional IRA and $30,000 a year from Social Security. Linda turns 73 this year, with a $500,000 IRA and her very first RMD. What you will learn: 1) RMDs start the year you reach age 73 on traditional IRAs and workplace plans, and a Roth IRA has no RMD while you are alive, 2) the formula: your December 31 balance divided by a factor from the IRS Uniform Lifetime Table (26.5 at 73, 24.6 at 75, 20.2 at 80, 16.0 at 85, 12.2 at 90), so Walter takes $800,000 / 24.6 = $32,520 and Linda takes $500,000 / 26.5 = $18,868, 3) the trap: your first RMD can wait until April 1 of the next year, but then you take two in the same year, like Linda's $18,868 + $20,000 = $38,868, 4) the penalty: 25% of the amount you did not take ($8,130 on Walter's RMD), or 10% if you fix it in time, reported on Form 5329, 5) how an RMD pulls your Social Security into the tax: Walter's income becomes $48,512 and his federal tax is $2,675, 6) the QCD: from age 70 and a half you can send money straight from your IRA to a charity, up to $108,000 in 2025 and $111,000 in 2026 per person, it counts toward your RMD and it is not counted as income, 7) Walter's $10,000 QCD cuts his tax to $586, saving $2,089, and the share of his Social Security that is taxed drops from 53% to 25% (the $10,000 goes to charity, so this works best if you already planned to give), 8) the QCD rules: the trustee must pay the charity directly, traditional IRAs only (not an ongoing SEP or SIMPLE IRA), get a receipt, no second deduction, and mark the QCD box on Form 1040, 9) several IRAs, 401(k)s, working past 73, and how withholding works (10% by default) with Form W-4R.
