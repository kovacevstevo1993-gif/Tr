## Titolo
Every Senior Must Know This Medigap Rule Before December 7 (Don't Drop It)

## Descrizione
Open enrollment ends December 7, and one choice can be very hard to undo: dropping a Medigap policy for a Medicare Advantage plan. Medicare itself warns that in most cases you may not be able to get that Medigap policy back, and in some cases you may not be able to get any Medigap policy at all. In this video you will see what Medigap and Plan G cover, what they do not, and the exact rules for buying, switching and getting protected. Every fact comes from Medicare and the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services.

We follow two fictional people. Carol turns 65 this spring and is choosing her very first Medicare coverage. Ben is 68, has a Medicare Advantage plan, and wonders whether to return to Original Medicare and add a Medigap plan. What you will learn: 1) Medigap, officially Medicare Supplement Insurance, is sold by private companies and helps pay your share of Original Medicare costs; Medicare does not pay anything toward the cost of the policy, 2) plans are standardized and named by letters (A, B, C, D, F, G, K, L, M, N; Massachusetts, Minnesota and Wisconsin differ), and the only difference between two Plan G policies is the price, 3) what Plan G covers: Part A coinsurance and hospital costs (up to 365 extra days), Part B coinsurance or copayment, the first 3 pints of blood, hospice and skilled nursing coinsurance, the Part A deductible, Part B excess charges, and 80% of foreign travel emergency care, 4) what Plan G does not cover: the Part B deductible ($283 in 2026), because Medigap plans sold to people new to Medicare on or after January 1, 2020 cannot cover it (Plans C and F are closed to them; Plans D and G are available), 5) what Medigap generally leaves out: long-term care, vision, dental, hearing aids, glasses, private-duty nursing, and drugs (you need a separate Part D plan); one policy covers one person, 6) the cost: a separate monthly premium on top of the Part B premium ($202.90 in 2026), and three ways to price a policy: community-rated, issue-age-rated and attained-age-rated, 7) the Medigap Open Enrollment Period: 6 months from the first month you have Part B and are 65 or older, one time only; during it a company cannot refuse you, use medical underwriting, or charge more for your health, 8) outside that window you may pay more, have fewer plans, and there is no guarantee a company will sell you a policy, 9) it is illegal to sell Medigap to someone in an Advantage plan unless they are switching back to Original Medicare, 10) guaranteed issue rights (for example when your Advantage plan leaves Medicare, stops giving care in your area, or you move out of its service area; apply no more than 63 days after coverage ends), 11) the two trial rights, 12) four steps to take this fall, including free help from your State Health Insurance Assistance Program.
