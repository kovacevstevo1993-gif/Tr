1 Aldi's own help center has a sentence that settles the senior discount question. In plain words: because Aldi offers the lowest possible prices, and the majority of its products are Aldi exclusive brands, it does not offer discounts at its store locations. No senior day, no age discount. And yet some videos promise hidden Aldi senior discounts. So I read Aldi's own help pages, and I found ten real things that can save you money, or save you a trip. Not one of them is a trick.
2 So here's what's coming. Ten things, all from Aldi's own pages. One is a small coin that comes back to you. One is a day of the week that decides when new items show up. One is a rule about your receipt that can cost you a refund. Another is the real cost of ordering online. And number ten is Aldi's strongest promise to shoppers, and you only get it if you do one simple thing before you leave the store. Stay for that one, because it's the one most worth knowing.
3 Is this video only for seniors? No, and I want to be honest about that. Nothing on Aldi's pages depends on your age. But if you're over sixty, a few of these matter more: how to avoid a wasted trip, how to carry less, and how to get your money back without an argument. Everything here comes from Aldi's help center and Aldi's corporate pages, and I'll tell you which one each time. Aldi doesn't date those pages, so the checkout screen is always your final answer.
4 Now, some of you are thinking, I don't shop at Aldi much. Fair enough. But these are the rules worth knowing before your first trip, and before a busy week. Others are thinking, I don't use apps, I just walk in and buy. Stay anyway. Most of this list works with no phone at all. Only number eight is about ordering online, and I'll tell you what it costs, so you can decide for yourself.
5 One thing before we start. Keep your receipt until you've checked it. You'll see why at number nine and number ten. And a quick word about money. Aldi says its prices can fluctuate from city to city, so I won't quote a single product price in this video. I'll only give you the rules, fees and policies that Aldi itself publishes, because those are the things you can check, and the things that don't change from one shelf to the next.
6 Number one. Exclusive brands. Aldi says that more than ninety percent of its product selection is Aldi exclusive brands, and that it guarantees the quality is as good as or better than name brands. On its corporate page, Aldi says its exclusive brands cost up to fifty percent less than national brands, with a footnote that this is based on price comparisons. That's Aldi's claim, not mine, so test it yourself. Pick one product you buy every week, and compare the two on the shelf.
7 And this is why the other savings tools you know don't work here. Aldi says it can't accept manufacturer coupons, because it already negotiates the best price with suppliers on the name brands it carries. It also says it doesn't offer rewards cards. Aldi coupons are printed and handed out in stores, or mailed to you, and a coupon you find online or on social media is not a real Aldi coupon. So don't print anything. Look at the shelf price instead.
8 Number two. A quarter and a bag. To unlock a cart at Aldi you insert a quarter, a twenty five cent deposit, and you get it back when you return the cart to the corral. Aldi says this saves it from hiring extra staff to collect carts. Then there are bags. Aldi says to bring your own shopping bags or buy a reusable Aldi bag. So keep a few quarters where you'll find them, and the bags by the door, so you don't leave them at home.
9 One small detail. Aldi's corporate page says shoppers pack their own groceries, which helps keep the lines moving faster. So plan for a minute or two at the end of the checkout, with your bags open and ready. And if lifting heavy bags is hard for you, number eight on this list is worth a look. Ordering online has a cost, and I'll tell you exactly what Aldi says it is.
10 Number three. Know when Aldi is closed. Aldi's help center says its stores are closed on New Year's Day, Easter Sunday, Thanksgiving Day and Christmas Day, and that stores keep limited hours on Memorial Day, Independence Day, Labor Day, Christmas Eve and New Year's Eve. The hours, services, payment options and weekly ad for your own store are on Aldi's store locator. A wasted trip costs gas and time, and this is an easy one to avoid.
11 And two things Aldi doesn't have. Its corporate page says its stores don't have add on services like banks and pharmacies, and its help center says there are no delis, although it sells fresh meats and cheeses in the deli products section. So if you take prescription medicine, you'll still need your own pharmacy. Plan your route so Aldi is one stop, not the only stop. Aldi also says its fresh produce is delivered daily.
12 Number four. Aldi Finds. These are limited time specialty items, and they change every week, from unique foods and home goods to things like gardening essentials and pet accessories. Aldi says they're available while supplies last, the stock varies by store, and it can't place holds or give rain checks. Most stores start the new Finds on Wednesdays, and Aldi posts them on its website a week early. For a Find you really want, Aldi recommends going to the store as soon as possible.
13 Aldi also says it uses Finds to test new products. If an item sells well, it could come back during the year, or even join the everyday selection. And here's a warning to remember when we reach the guarantee. Aldi leaves non food Finds out of it. So if a gadget or a gardening tool doesn't work out, you may be stuck with it. Think twice before you buy a Find on impulse, and ask yourself if you would still want it at the register.
14 Number five. The weekly ad and the Wednesday email. Aldi has no coupons and no rewards card, so its own help center points you to the Weekly Specials to save more. The weekly email always goes out on a Wednesday, and you can look at the weekly ad on Aldi's website, or on your store's page in the store locator. Aldi's Weekly Fresh Meat Specials work the same way as the Finds: limited time, and while supplies last.
15 Here's an easy way to use it. Before you go, open the ad for your store, write a short list on paper, and add the Finds you really want. Then you're not wandering the aisles and grabbing extras. That's not an Aldi rule, it's just a habit. But Aldi says its stores are designed to make shopping smart, fast and easy, and a list helps with all three, especially when your feet get tired.
16 Number six. How you pay. In the store, Aldi accepts cash, most debit cards, Visa, Mastercard, Discover, American Express, E B T, snap, Link cards, and tap payments like Apple Pay and Google Pay. It also offers cash back of ten, twenty, forty or one hundred dollars with qualifying cards. But Aldi says that at this time it does not accept checks or W I C. So if you still pay by check, bring a card or some cash with you, because the cashier can't take the check.
17 If you get snap benefits, all Aldi stores accept them, and online they work at participating pickup and delivery locations. Snap only pays for eligible food, and online it can't pay for tips or fees, so you'll need a debit or credit card for those. On Aldi's website, eligible products have a blue badge. And if you're not sure what counts, the U S D A keeps the official list of snap eligible foods on its website, f n s dot u s d a dot gov.
18 Number seven. Aldi gift cards. Aldi says they come in five dollar steps up to one hundred dollars, only in its stores, with no fees, and the balance does not expire. You can use part of the balance, and what's left shows at the bottom of your receipt. Two limits to know. They can't be used for online orders, and if a card is lost or stolen, Aldi says it can't be replaced. So treat a gift card like cash, and keep it somewhere safe.
19 Number eight. Ordering online, and what it really costs. Aldi's delivery and pickup run through Instacart. Aldi says orders must be at least ten dollars to qualify, pickup fees start at one dollar and ninety nine cents, and delivery fees depend on the delivery window and the order total. You also need an Aldi account, because there's no guest ordering, and the account itself has no fee. Bags are charged too, a small fee, just like in the store. And tips aren't accepted on curbside orders.
20 The part to read twice is the prices. Aldi's help center says that prices online may vary slightly from the prices in the store, that some promotions in the store may not apply to online orders, and that online items may be priced higher to cover the cost of shopping or delivery. So it's not the same basket. It can still be the right choice if carrying groceries is hard, or the weather is bad. Just choose it knowing the extra cost, and look at the total before you pay.
21 And if something is wrong with an online order, Aldi says to tell Instacart within three days of pickup or delivery, if items are missing, damaged or spoiled. Refunds on online orders go through Instacart, and Aldi says it does not accept returns or exchanges for items bought online. That's a different system from the one in the store, and the store system is what the next two numbers are about.
22 Number nine. Your receipt. If you think an item was scanned wrong or you were overcharged, Aldi says to bring the receipt back to the store and ask for the manager on duty. And if a second charge shows up on your account after you pay with a card, Aldi says a temporary pending charge can appear after a digital payment, and it should drop off within ten business days, depending on your bank. So look at the receipt before you leave the parking lot, not at home.
23 Why am I telling you to keep the receipt? Because of what comes next. Aldi says a refund in the original form of payment requires a receipt. Without one, you receive an Aldi merchandise credit gift card for the current retail price of the item, which may not match what you paid. So keep the receipt in your bag until the product is gone. A receipt is the difference between getting your money back and getting store credit.
24 Number ten. The Twice as Nice Guarantee. Aldi says that if for any reason you're not one hundred percent satisfied with the quality of any product, it will replace the product and refund your money. Both, not one or the other. You bring the packaging and any unused product to your local Aldi store manager. It applies to purchases made in the store only. And it's not a senior perk, because nothing in the policy depends on age. It applies to every shopper.
25 Now the fine print, because it matters. Aldi lists what is left out: non food Aldi Finds, replacements from an earlier claim, and problems that have nothing to do with quality. If you return several units of the same product for the same quality problem, Aldi may refund all of them, but you get only one replacement. So don't wait until the box is empty. If something tastes wrong, bring it back with what's left and your receipt.
