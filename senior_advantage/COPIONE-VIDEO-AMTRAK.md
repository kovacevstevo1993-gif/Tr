1 Two people over sixty five ride the same Amtrak train, on the same day, in the same kind of seat. One of them paid less, never lifted a suitcase, and knew how to cancel without losing money. The other one paid full price and carried every bag alone. The only difference? One of them asked.
2 Today I'll show you ten things Amtrak gives riders over sixty five, and riders who need a little help, when they ask. One saves you money before you leave home. One carries your bags for you. And number ten is a piece of paper from your doctor that can save the whole ticket.
3 Now, some of you are thinking, I don't take the train. Others are thinking, I already know about the senior discount. Stay with me. Many people who know about the discount still lose money on the ticket, and I'll show you exactly where.
4 And if you've ever stood on a platform with a heavy bag, wondering how you'd get it up those steps, this video is for you.
5 Stay until the end, because there is one sentence that unlocks almost all of these. And most riders only say it when it's already too late.
6 Everything here comes from Amtrak's own pages, on amtrak dot com. No rumors. I'm not paid by Amtrak, and rules change, so always check before you book.
7 Number one. The senior discount. If you're sixty five or older, Amtrak gives you ten percent off most rail fares, on most trains.
8 Here's the mistake. The discount doesn't appear by itself. When you search for a ticket, you have to choose Senior as the traveler type. If you leave it on Adult, you pay the adult price.
9 Two more things. It does not apply to the charge for a sleeping room, only to the rail fare. And you need proof of age, when you buy the ticket and again on the train. So keep your I D in your pocket, not in your suitcase. And if you ride a train that crosses into Canada with VIA Rail, the senior discount there starts at sixty.
10 It also may not combine with other offers, so compare. Sometimes a sale fare is cheaper than the discount. Your action is simple. Every time you book, choose Senior, then compare the prices before you pay.
11 Number two. Twenty four hours to change your mind. If you book a reserved ticket and cancel within twenty four hours of buying it, before the train leaves, Amtrak gives you a full refund to your original payment.
12 Who does this help? Anyone who books in a hurry, books for a grandchild, or isn't sure about the date. Book the price you like, then check your calendar calmly at home. You have one full day. Just know that for unreserved tickets, the window is only one hour.
13 Number three. The fare you pick decides what you get back. And this is where many seniors lose money without knowing it.
14 With a Flex fare, you can cancel before the train leaves and get all your money back, with no fee. You can also change it with no fee. You only pay the difference in price.
15 With a Value fare, the cheaper one, you can't change it. If you cancel before departure, you get seventy percent back. So on a one hundred dollar ticket, you get seventy dollars, and thirty are gone. With a Sale fare, you get only half back.
16 Now the trap. If you don't show up, and you don't cancel before the train leaves, you lose the whole value of that trip. Every dollar. Even on a Flex fare.
17 So here's your action. If your plans might change, a doctor's visit, the weather, your health, look at the Flex price before you choose. And if you can't go, cancel online, in the app, or by phone, before the train leaves. Never just stay home.
18 Number four. Your bags. On Amtrak, each traveler can bring one personal item and two carry on bags, at no charge. The personal item can weigh up to twenty five pounds and has to fit under the seat. Each carry on can weigh up to fifty pounds.
19 And on trains with checked baggage, two checked bags are free. Extra bags cost twenty dollars each. Compare that with your last flight.
20 The mistake is bringing one bag too many, or one that's too big. That's twenty dollars for each extra piece. Your action: before you leave, count your bags. One small, two medium, and the big ones checked.
21 Number five. Someone to carry your bags. At many big stations, Amtrak has Red Caps. They carry your luggage, and Amtrak calls this help free.
22 You'll find them at twelve major stations, like Chicago, New York, Washington, Philadelphia, Boston, Los Angeles and Seattle. They wear red shirts and red caps. Only accept help from someone in that uniform.
23 Every bag they handle gets a claim check, so keep that ticket safe. A tip is welcome, but the service itself costs nothing. And at a number of stations, Amtrak also has self service handcarts. Your action: look for the red cap, and simply say, can you help me with my bags?
24 Number six. Help getting to your train. If walking far or stairs are hard for you, you can ask Amtrak for help at the station. A station wheelchair, or a staff member who takes you to your train.
25 The best way is to ask when you make your reservation. You can do it on amtrak dot com, or by phone at one, eight hundred, U S A, rail. Most staffed stations have courtesy wheelchairs.
26 Then arrive at least one hour before your train, and tell an Amtrak agent you're there. They also help you to and from the restroom, and with stairs. The mistake is arriving ten minutes early and hoping someone notices you. Nobody will guess.
27 Number seven. The things you must reserve before you go. If you need a ramp or a lift to get on the train, Amtrak asks you to reserve it. And if you need an accessible seat, even without a wheelchair, you can reserve that too.
28 Here's the catch. These seats are limited. Amtrak says to book early to make sure one is available. The person who books early gets the seat. The person who asks at the platform may find none left.
29 The same goes for oxygen. Amtrak allows it for passengers with a disability, but you can't add it on the website. You call one, eight hundred, U S A, rail, and reserve in advance. Your machine must run at least four hours without power from the train, so check your batteries.
30 Number eight. Ice for your medicine. If you take medication that must stay cool, Amtrak says it can give you ice for your cooler or container. Most riders never ask.
31 And keep every pill with you, never in a checked bag. You can't reach checked bags during the trip, and you must be able to take your medication by yourself. Your action: a small cooler, and one sentence to the crew. Could I get some ice for my medicine?
32 Number nine. Your own food. You're allowed to bring your own food and drinks on board, and eat them at your seat or in your private room.
33 Why does this matter? Amtrak itself warns that trains are sometimes delayed, and food supplies can run low. If you have diabetes or a special diet, it suggests carrying enough food for a delay. A sandwich, some fruit, crackers. Pack it at home.
34 One rule people break. Your own alcohol can only be enjoyed inside a private sleeping room, never in the public areas. And food you buy in the dining car is eaten in the dining car. Also, the crew can't heat or refrigerate your own food, so bring things that travel well.
35 Number ten. The one almost nobody knows. A note from your doctor.
36 Remember the Value fare, the one you can't change, where you lose part of the money if you cancel? If you get sick, Amtrak's rule says that with a note from your doctor, it waives the change and cancellation fees for the sick passenger and the people traveling with them.
37 Think about what that means. You booked the cheaper ticket for you and your partner. A week before the trip, you get the flu, or you have a fall. Without the note, part of that money is gone. With the note, those fees go away for both of you.
38 The mistake is staying home and saying nothing. Remember, if you don't cancel before the train leaves, you lose the whole trip. So the moment you know you can't go, call Amtrak before departure, say you're sick, and ask what they need from your doctor.
