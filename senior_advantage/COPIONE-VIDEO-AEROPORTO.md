1 At an airport security line, there is a rule written only for people seventy five and older. And the people it was written for often walk right past it, because nobody at the checkpoint says a word.
2 I'm talking about eight things. Your photo I D. Help from the curb to your gate. Your cane. Your oxygen machine. T S A Cares. The seventy five rule. Your pills. And one rule about a cancelled flight that can put real money back in your pocket. The biggest one is number eight.
3 Now, some of you are thinking, I don't need any help at the airport. Others are thinking, I only fly once a year, so this isn't for me. Both are missing something, and I'll show you what in a minute.
4 And if you've ever stood in a security line wondering if you were doing something wrong, stay with me. Most of these take thirty seconds to use, and they cost nothing.
5 Stay until the end, because there is one sentence that makes almost all of these work. And most people only say it after the problem has already happened.
6 One more thing before we start. Everything in this video comes from official pages, T S A dot gov and the federal rules for airlines. No guesses, no rumors. Rules change, so confirm before you travel.
7 Number one. Your photo I D. Since May seventh, twenty twenty five, to fly inside the country, you need a Real I D, or a passport.
8 Look at your driver's license right now. A star in the corner, or the word Enhanced, means it counts. No star, and no passport, and you have a problem at the checkpoint.
9 The common mistake is thinking your old license is fine because it hasn't expired. The expiration date isn't the point. The star is.
10 T S A lists a forty five dollar fee for travelers who show up without an acceptable I D. It is much easier to check the card at home.
11 Who does this hit hardest? Older drivers who haven't renewed in years, and people who never needed a passport. You get a Real I D through your state's motor vehicle office, so don't wait until the week of your trip.
12 So here's your action. Take out your license tonight. Look for the star. If it's not there, call your state motor vehicle office.
13 Number two. Help getting through the airport. Federal rules say that if you have a disability, the airline must provide or arrange help moving from the terminal entrance to your gate, and from your gate back out.
14 That includes help making a connection between two gates. It includes a short stop at a restroom on the way, if you ask. And if you can't carry your bags because of your disability, the airline has to help with the luggage.
15 Now, here is the honest part. These rules are about disability, not about your birthday. Being over sixty is not enough by itself. But if walking far is hard, or standing for a long time is hard, this is for you.
16 And the wheelchair service comes from the airline, not from T S A. People mix this up, then stand at the checkpoint wondering why nobody showed up.
17 Here's what many people don't know. The federal rule says an airline must not require advance notice for the services it is required to give you. So if you need help on the day, you are allowed to ask on the day.
18 Even so, ask when you book. A request in your reservation makes the day smoother. And you have to say that you need the help, because nobody at the airport will guess.
19 So say it plainly, at booking or at the counter. I need help from the curb to my gate.
20 Number three. Your cane, your walker, your wheelchair. They are not luggage.
21 The federal rule says airlines must not count these toward your carry on limit. Canes, crutches, walkers, a folding wheelchair. As long as they fit in the priority storage area, the overhead bin, or under a seat.
22 The common mistake is paying a bag fee, or arguing at the gate about the carry on limit, for something that was never supposed to count.
23 And if your wheelchair or mobility device has to be taken apart to be stored, the airline must put it back together, and return it promptly, in the condition it was received.
24 If it comes back damaged, take a photo before you leave, and report it to the airline right there at the airport.
25 So, who does this help? Anyone who flies with a cane or a walker. Your action is one sentence at the gate. This is my mobility aid, it doesn't count as my carry on.
26 Number four. One of only two things on this list that need advance notice.
27 If you use a portable oxygen concentrator, a ventilator, a respirator, or a C P A P machine on the plane, federal rules let the airline ask you for forty eight hours notice.
28 That is the exception, not the rule. And here's the mistake. People arrive with the machine, assume it's allowed, and learn at the gate that the airline needed to know two days earlier.
29 Not every airline allows every oxygen model, either. T S A says to check with your airline and the maker of your device before you fly.
30 Who does this help? Anyone who sleeps with a machine, or breathes with help. Your action is a phone call when you book. Ask, what do you need from me, and by when?
31 Number five. A T S A helper for the security checkpoint. It's called T S A Cares.
32 If you want a trained T S A officer to help you through screening, you contact T S A Cares at least seventy two hours before you leave. The number is eight five five, seven eight seven, two two two seven.
33 They answer on weekdays from eight in the morning to eleven at night Eastern time, and on weekends and holidays from nine in the morning to eight at night.
34 Now, what it is not. It does not get you a wheelchair, that's the airline. It does not get you a faster line, that's T S A PreCheck. And you are still screened.
35 Who does it help? Someone who has trouble following instructions, who has a medical device, or who carries medically necessary liquids. Your action is a call, at least three days before you fly.
36 Number six. If you are seventy five or older, security has special steps for you. And if you are younger, this one is for your parents.
37 T S A says passengers seventy five and older may keep their shoes on. A light jacket does have to come off for the body scanner.
38 But here is the catch. If an alarm goes off, an officer may still ask you to take off your shoes, or give you a pat down. Keeping your shoes on is the starting point, not a guarantee.
39 If you're seventy five or older and you use a wheelchair or a scooter, you may stay seated in a standard lane. The officer will test your hands for explosives traces.
40 And if you can't stand during screening, you can ask for a chair.
41 The mistake is taking your shoes off automatically, just because everyone around you does. Your action is one question at the checkpoint. I'm seventy five or older, may I keep my shoes on?
42 Number seven. Your medications at the checkpoint.
43 Pills have to go through screening like everything else. Clear labels help it go faster. They may be looked at, or tested for traces.
44 T S A says to tell the officer before screening begins that you have medically necessary liquids or medications, and to put them in a separate bin.
45 And T S A allows larger amounts of medically necessary liquids, gels and aerosols, in reasonable quantities for your flight. You declare them, and take them out of your bag.
46 If you don't want a liquid opened or X rayed, you can ask. They will use other steps instead, like a body scan or a pat down.
47 The common mistake is saying nothing. Then it alarms, and now it's a longer search. Your action is simple. Tell the officer first, and put it in its own bin.
48 Now a bonus. At any moment during screening, you can ask for a private screening, and have a witness of your choice with you.
49 And if something goes wrong at the checkpoint, ask for a supervisor right there. Afterward, you can contact the T S A Contact Center at eight six six, two eight nine, nine six seven three.
50 Now number eight, the one I promised. The biggest one. A cancelled flight.
51 When an airline cancels your flight, or changes it a lot, and you don't take what they offer instead, federal rules say you are owed your money back, as a refund. Not a voucher.
52 A voucher is allowed only if you choose it. It can't be pushed on you. You are allowed to say no.
53 The refund also covers the taxes and the extra fees you paid.
54 The common mistake is saying yes to the first thing offered at the counter, because you're tired, and the line is long.
55 Who does this help? Anyone who books ahead, and a cancelled flight is exactly when it matters. Your action is one sentence. I'd like a refund to my original payment method, not a voucher.
